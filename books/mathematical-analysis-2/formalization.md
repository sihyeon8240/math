# Mathematical Analysis II: formalization boundary

This document owns this book's mathematical prerequisites under the
[repository Lean policy](../../docs/formalization.md), which governs standard
interfaces, independent proofs, and citations. These assumptions apply only to
this book.

## Starting structures

Accept classical logic, elementary sets and functions, induction and
well-ordering, finite/countable indexing, and arithmetic in the usual number
systems. Accept the real numbers as a complete ordered field and standard
finite-dimensional real and complex vector spaces.

## Accepted results

- First-course analysis through metric topology, compactness and connectedness,
  numerical sequences and series, continuity, one-variable differentiation, and
  Riemann-Stieltjes integration, including the fundamental theorem of calculus.
  Measure-theoretic convergence theorems are not included.
- Finite-dimensional linear algebra through bases, dimension, linear maps and
  matrices, rank-nullity, determinants, inner products, and orthogonalization.
  Finite-dimensional norm equivalence and continuity of linear maps are accepted
  for multivariable analysis.
- Elementary real and complex polynomial algebra through evaluation, degree
  rules, division, and the factor theorem.

These results may be used whether or not the other textbooks have formalized
them. Later additions to Analysis I do not automatically expand this boundary.

## Results developed in this book

Develop function sequences and series, uniform convergence and interchange of
limits, differentiation and integration, special functions, multivariable
differentiation and inverse/implicit function theorems, integration of
differential forms, and Lebesgue theory.

Measure construction, measurable functions, Lebesgue integration, and its
convergence theorems are core developments. Standard measure and integral
representations do not authorize using their substantive properties as proof
shortcuts; different integral notions require a proved correspondence.

## Scoped external exceptions

None. General measure theory, functional analysis, manifold theory, and general
Stokes theorems are not blanket prerequisites.
