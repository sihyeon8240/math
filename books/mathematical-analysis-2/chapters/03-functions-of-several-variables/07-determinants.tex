Determinants are assumed from finite-dimensional linear algebra. For a linear
map $A:\R^{n}\to\R^{n}$, $\det A$ denotes the determinant of its matrix in the
standard basis. We use multilinearity, alternation, $\det I=1$, and
\[
  \det(AB)=\det A\,\det B,
  \qquad A\text{ is invertible}\iff\det A\neq 0.
\]
No reconstruction of determinant theory is needed here. In multivariable
analysis, the determinant of the derivative expresses the nonsingularity
condition. Its role in change of variables is developed with integration;
the change-of-variables theorem is not an assumed linear algebra result.
