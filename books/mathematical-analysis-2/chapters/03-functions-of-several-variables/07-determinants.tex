Determinants are assumed from finite-dimensional linear algebra. For a linear
map $A:\R^{n}\to\R^{n}$, $\det A$ denotes the determinant of its matrix in the
standard basis. We use multilinearity, alternation, $\det I=1$, and
\[
  \det(AB)=\det A\,\det B,
  \qquad A\text{ is invertible}\iff\det A\neq 0.
\]
The derivative is nonsingular when its determinant is nonzero.
Its role in the change-of-variables theorem is developed in the chapter on integration.
